# Owner interviews and timing study

Feedback we got: *talk to 5 kirana owners before building more*, and *kiranas already deliver over WhatsApp, so what exactly is missing?* This guide answers both with evidence instead of assumptions. Bring the PADOSI app on a phone; the timing study runs inside it.

## Who to talk to (5 conversations)

| # | Type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Existing kirana owners who already take WhatsApp orders | Tests the original thesis: is reading, pricing and replying to lists a real time cost? |
| 4–5 | New store entrepreneurs (opened in the last 1–2 years, or about to open) | Tests the Jumbotail lesson: are new owners more open to new tools than long-time owners? |

Mix areas (e.g. one in an apartment-heavy area like HSR/Bellandur, one in an older area like Jayanagar/Malleshwaram). Visit mid-morning or mid-afternoon, never during the evening rush.

## Questions (20 minutes, listen more than talk)

Don't pitch PADOSI until the last section. Ask about the last real order, not about "usually".

**Their day and WhatsApp orders**
1. How do customers order from you apart from walking in? Roughly how many WhatsApp orders yesterday?
2. Show me the last WhatsApp order you got. What did you do with it, step by step?
3. Who replies with the total? How do you work out the prices: memory, a book, a calculator?
4. What goes wrong with WhatsApp orders? (wrong item, missed message, price dispute, unpaid)
5. When you're busy at the counter, what happens to WhatsApp messages?

**Losing customers**
6. Have you lost regular customers to Blinkit, Zepto or Instamart? How do you know?
7. Roughly how much has monthly sales changed in the last two years? *(This is how we source or replace the ₹90k figure. Write down the exact words and the number.)*

**Tools they've tried**
8. Have you used Dunzo, an ONDC app, Vyapar, Khatabook, Dukaan, or a distributor app (e.g. Jumbotail, Udaan)? What happened? Why did you stop?
9. Who set it up? How long did adding your products take?

**Only now: show PADOSI**
10. Paste one of their real WhatsApp lists into the app (or photograph it). Watch silently. What do they say?
11. Would you use this every day? What would stop you?
12. Would you pay ₹____ a month for it? Who in the shop would use it?

## Timing study (the number the pitch needs)

The scope doc says the MVP must prove an order can be quoted in **about a minute**. For each owner, time 2 real orders:

1. **By hand:** give the owner a real list and ask them to reply with a priced total the way they normally do. Start the stopwatch in the app's **Field test** box, and stop it when the reply is ready.
2. **With PADOSI:** paste the same list into **New quote**, fix anything flagged, approve. The app records the time automatically.
3. Save the hand time on the approved quote. The **Orders** tab shows the average for both.

Target: 10 orders across 3+ stores. If PADOSI doesn't get an order under about a minute, or doesn't save at least 3 minutes over doing it by hand, the impact case doesn't hold. Say so honestly in the pitch.

## What would change our mind

- Owners say WhatsApp orders are few (<3 a day): the time saving is too small, so focus on reorder reminders to win lapsed regulars instead.
- Owners already reply in seconds from memory: the pain is elsewhere (delivery, credit, stock), so re-scope.
- Only new entrepreneurs show interest: make them the launch customer, with onboarding from supplier bills as the hook.
